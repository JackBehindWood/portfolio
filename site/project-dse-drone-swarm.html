DSE drone swarm for wildfire detection

team project · BSc, 10 weeks, 10 students (Design Synthesis Exercise, AE3200)

Design of a UAV swarm that monitors forests, detects fires and collects environmental data for a wildfire ignition and spread model. Team of ten over ten weeks. Role: connecting sub-teams, and UAV design work on stability and control, aerodynamics, propulsion and power, flight performance and verification and validation. The client was an external research institute; the swarm monitors forests and collects environmental data.

Method

  • Mission and requirements: mission statement, concept description and requirement strategy.
  • UAV design: flight performance, aerodynamics, stability and control, and propulsion and power, combined in an iterated design loop.
  • Swarm: each UAV covers a sector of the risk area; the framework was tested in simulation.
  • Verification and validation: verification and validation plan, assumptions and model checks.

Limits

Design study only: no prototype was built or flown.

Key facts

  • In the DSE Jack worked on the UAV design: stability and control, aerodynamics, propulsion and power, flight performance and verification and validation; also mission definition and requirements
  • Team of ten students; external research institute as client; UAV swarm for wildfire monitoring and data collection
  • The team's swarm planning gives each UAV a sector of the search area with a pre-defined path; the framework was validated by simulating a 20-UAV surveillance swarm
  • The final UAV design has a maximum take-off mass of 14.59 kg, lithium-ion powered, sized by iterating the subsystem estimates
  • The design iterator was checked with a convergence and sensitivity study and verified and validated against an existing UAV design
  • Communications architecture is distributed and decentralised, with a relay drone and a link margin above 5 dB on all links
