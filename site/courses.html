AE2235 · BSc TU Delft · 7 EC

Aerospace Signals, Systems and Control

Two linked parts. The first covers control theory for aerospace systems: describing a system as a state-space model, transfer function or block diagram, judging its stability and response, and choosing and tuning a controller (P, PI, PD, PID, lead and lag) with Bode, Nyquist and root-locus methods. Examples range from whole aircraft and satellites to components such as a control surface with a hydraulic servo.

The second covers signal analysis: Fourier series and transforms, spectral density, linear time-invariant systems, filtering, sampling and aliasing, the DFT and FFT, and how noise limits what can be detected. It also introduces signal detection and communication links.

The control design was done in Python.

  • Python
  • Signal analysis
  • Fourier analysis
  • Filtering and sampling
  • Control theory
