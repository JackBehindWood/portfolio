About

I am an aerospace engineer who likes problems that sit between fields.

Where it started

Flight has fascinated me since I was a child, so I studied Aerospace Engineering at TU Delft (BSc, 2021 to 2024). I also enjoy maths and hard modelling problems.

Widening the lens

During my BSc I spent a semester at Universidad Carlos III de Madrid, taking Business Administration courses. I then did the MSc in Sustainable Air Transport.

I wanted to see real operations, not just theory, and my internship at KLM Cargo's Cargo Control Center was my first look. I built a forecasting baseline there and wrote the mathematical design for ULD allocation. The details are confidential. The KLM project.

Where AI meets optimisation

My thesis sits there: a planner assigns runways and arrival times, and a reinforcement learning agent adapts the route to meet them. I evaluated it across 600,000 simulated aircraft. The thesis.

My optimisation work beyond that is in university coursework: I used Gurobi in university projects, including column generation in group coursework. The courses.

Building things

At Juvoly I am the sole developer of a wireless audio recorder prototype for clinical consultations, in C++ on an ESP32. On my GitHub there are public repositories such as nexahome (a modular, self-hosted homelab platform), Oryx (an open-source engine for games, strategies, simulation and decision-making) and Ignis (a real-time explosion simulator in C++).

With people

Since November 2021 I have tutored maths, physics and chemistry. I love teaching, and explaining something shows me how well I understand it. In the design project of my BSc I connected the sub-teams of a team of ten. I love discussing with people and using everyone's experience and ideas to make the best possible product.

I want to be challenged. PowerPoint and Excel are tools I have developed over many years.

When it went wrong

In my thesis, adding heading information to the observation made success collapse to 77% on closely spaced parallel runways. The cause was approach direction, with late detours. The final model leaves that information out, with no loss in performance. More on the thesis.
