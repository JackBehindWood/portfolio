Trajectory-Based Aircraft Landing Problem (TBALP)

research · 2026 (exam 2026-10-13)

MSc thesis, TU Delft, Sustainable Air Transport. A hierarchical reinforcement learning framework for the landing problem at an airport.

Previous reinforcement learning arrival models treat the airport as a single sink, so traffic concentrates on one runway while others idle (Runway Overload). This thesis couples spatial and temporal arrival management to address that.

Method

  • Manager: Constrained Position Shifting planner assigns runways and arrival times.
  • Surrogate: Extra Trees model gives fast ETA estimates to the manager.
  • Worker: Soft Actor-Critic agent adapts the route to meet the assigned time.
  • Tools: Python, BlueSky-gym (Gymnasium), Stable-Baselines3 with hindsight replay, PyTorch.
  • Setup: trained and evaluated across all twelve runway ends of Amsterdam Schiphol.
  • Surrogate choice: Extra Trees had the lowest error of ten candidate regressors.
  • Phases: two training phases and a coordination-evaluation phase.
  • Selection and checks: statistical tests (Diebold-Mariano, Wilcoxon, Mann-Whitney, Welch, Holm-Bonferroni) and key metrics computed by two independent paths.

Limits

No exact optimiser (MILP) was built: MILP and dynamic programming appear in the literature review only. Homogeneous A320 fleet, deterministic environment, one airport, heading control only.

Key facts

  • Evaluated across 600,000 simulated aircraft (BlueSky-gym, Schiphol configuration)
  • Over 99.8% success
  • Mean timing error under 12 seconds (under 0.2 minute)
  • Adding heading information collapsed success on closely spaced parallel runways to 77%; cause traced to approach direction (late detours); the final model omits that information with no performance loss
  • Implemented on Gymnasium-compatible BlueSky-gym, with Stable-Baselines3 SAC and a hindsight (HER) replay buffer for goal-conditioned training
  • Trained and evaluated across all twelve runway ends of Amsterdam Schiphol, not a two-runway simplification
  • Extra Trees gave the lowest error of ten candidate regressors for the arrival-time surrogate; Random Forest was runner-up
  • Two training phases (spatial, then spatio-temporal) and a coordination-evaluation phase
  • Model and policy selection used statistical tests (Diebold-Mariano, Wilcoxon signed-rank, Mann-Whitney, Welch, Holm-Bonferroni correction)
  • Key metrics (separation compliance, throughput) were computed by two independent paths and cross-checked; a capacity floor was derived independently with Little's Law
  • About 99.9% on time
  • Two deadlocks in 600,000 aircraft, both recovered
  • Runway Overload is no longer reproduced in the evaluation
  • MILP and dynamic programming appear in the thesis only as literature-review benchmarks. No solver was used in the thesis
  • The thesis does not report a CPLEX, Gurobi or OR-Tools model
  • Literature review covered the aircraft landing problem: exact methods (MILP, dynamic programming), heuristics and metaheuristics, decision-support tools and reinforcement learning
  • Coordination results were compared with the runway-overload behaviour reported for a prior published single-sink multi-agent model
  • Dynamic runway assignment gave no advantage under uniform demand; two alternative penalty terms were tested and rejected
  • In the full evaluation the scheduled pass tracked its assigned arrival time roughly 18 to 25 times more accurately than a static pass, across six conditions
  • A research direction (critic as runtime safety monitor) was dropped before producing results
  • A throughput metric was corrected during the work (denominator included transit time) and re-verified
