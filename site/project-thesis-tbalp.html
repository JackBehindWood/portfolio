Trajectory-Based Aircraft Landing Problem (TBALP)

Hierarchical reinforcement learning for aircraft landings, trained and evaluated across all twelve runway ends of Amsterdam Schiphol.

  • Python
  • PyTorch
  • Stable-Baselines3
  • BlueSky-gym
  • Gymnasium

MSc thesis, TU Delft, Sustainable Air Transport. A hierarchical reinforcement learning framework for the landing problem at an airport.

Previous reinforcement learning arrival models treat the airport as a single sink, so traffic concentrates on one runway while others idle (Runway Overload). This thesis couples spatial and temporal arrival management to address that.

Approach

  • Manager: Constrained Position Shifting planner assigns runways and arrival times.
  • Surrogate: Extra Trees model gives fast ETA estimates to the manager.
  • Worker: Soft Actor-Critic agent adapts the route to meet the assigned time.
  • Tools: Python, BlueSky-gym (Gymnasium), Stable-Baselines3 with hindsight replay, PyTorch.
  • Setup: trained and evaluated across all twelve runway ends of Amsterdam Schiphol.
  • Surrogate choice: Extra Trees had the lowest error of ten candidate regressors.
  • Phases: two training phases and a coordination-evaluation phase.
  • Selection and checks: statistical tests (Diebold-Mariano, Wilcoxon, Mann-Whitney, Welch, Holm-Bonferroni) and key metrics computed by two independent paths.

Results

Evaluated across 600,000 simulated aircraft on the Schiphol configuration: over 99.8% success and a mean timing error under 12 seconds. The scheduled pass tracked its assigned arrival time roughly 18 to 25 times more accurately than a static pass, across six conditions.

Coordination no longer reproduces the runway overload reported for a prior published single-sink multi-agent model.

What did not work

  • Adding heading information to the observation collapsed success on closely spaced parallel runways; the final model omits it with no loss.
  • Dynamic runway assignment gave no advantage under uniform demand.
  • A research direction using the critic as a runtime safety monitor was dropped before it produced results.
  • A throughput metric was found to be biased by transit time, corrected and re-verified.

Limits

No exact optimiser (MILP) was built: MILP and dynamic programming appear in the literature review only. Homogeneous A320 fleet, deterministic environment, one airport, heading control only.
